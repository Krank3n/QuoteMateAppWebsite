## 0. Read this before you paste anything

Three constraints that shaped the copy:

1. **No supplier names.** Per the no-supplier-names rule, this copy says
   "major Australian suppliers" rather than naming any retailer. Directory
   listings are permanent, syndicated, and scraped — the wrong place to test
   whether a supplier objects.
2. **No invented scale.** We have a handful of paying subscribers. Nothing
   below claims user counts, "trusted by thousands", or awards. Directories
   are exactly where a false claim gets noticed, and an LLM that catches one
   wrong fact discounts the whole entity.
3. **No founding-price framing.** The site shows $49/$328 as a capped
   founding rate that reverts to $99/$658. A directory listing can't update
   itself when the cap fills, so it states the current price plainly with no
   scarcity language. Diarise a review when the cap closes.

QuoteMate is a quoting-first app for Australian trade businesses — sole traders and small crews who mainly need to price work accurately, send it fast, and get paid.

## 8. The part that actually determines whether this works

A directory listing with zero reviews is close to invisible — G2 and Capterra
rank by review volume and recency, and answer engines quote the ranked lists,
not the raw profile pages. Creating the listing is maybe an hour; earning the
first reviews is the real task.

Honest constraint: we have a small paying base, so this is a hand-run play,
not a campaign.

**Sequence:**

1. Create the Capterra/GetApp listing first — it's the easiest approval and
   feeds the whole G2-owned network from one submission.
2. Claim the vendor profile so you can respond to reviews. Vendor responses
   are themselves crawlable text and read as an active, real company.
3. Ask every current paying subscriber directly, by name, one at a time. A
   personal message from the founder converts far better than any automated
   prompt, and at this volume automation isn't worth building.
4. Ask the two people who left the detailed Google Play reviews (Apr 2026) —
   they already wrote long, specific, positive reviews unprompted, which
   makes them the most likely to write another.
5. Target **five reviews** as the first milestone. Capterra begins showing
   star ratings and including products in category pages around that point;
   below it the listing exists but doesn't compete.

**Do not** offer payment, discounts, or gift cards for reviews. G2 and
Capterra both police incentivised reviews, removal is public, and a
review-fraud flag attached to the brand is precisely the kind of signal that
would follow us into every AI answer about the product.
