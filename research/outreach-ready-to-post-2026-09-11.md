> A decade on from this thread, and the $3.5k–$9k desktop estimating packages it's complaining about are still around, but they're no longer the only option for a builder who mostly needs to price a reno and get it out the door. Disclosure first: I build a quoting app for tradies, so read this as one opinion from inside the industry.
>
> What changed is the phone. Estimating used to mean a desktop, a takeoff from plans and a licence that cost more than a ute. For a residential builder quoting from a site visit rather than a plan set, that's the wrong tool. The job is: itemise the materials, price them at what you'll actually pay this week, add labour and margin, put PC items and provisional sums on it for anything the client hasn't chosen, set a 30-day validity, and send it before you leave. That is a two-minute job on a phone now, and a spreadsheet can't do the "what you'll actually pay this week" part.
>
> Where the desktop packages still earn their money is takeoffs off drawings. If you build whole houses from architectural plans, you want estimating software that measures quantities off the PDF; Buildxact is the Australian one most builders name. If you build bathrooms, decks, extensions and granny flats from a site visit, you want a quoting app and you'll never open a takeoff tool.
>
> Three things I'd tell anyone in this thread's position today: price off live supplier data, not a list you built last quarter, because that's where the margin goes; put staged payments on the quote tied to milestones, not dates; and pick whichever tool lets you send the quote from the driveway, because the builder who quotes same-day wins the job more often than the one who quotes cheapest.

## 3. Whirlpool — thread 2769852 (advice only, no links, no product)

https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/thread/2769852 — verified open 2026-09-11,
16 posts, last Dec 2018. Register openly; QuoteMate in profile field only.

OP wanted invoices that show a total with a job description rather than
itemised lines. Answer the actual problem.

> Late to this one, but the problem in the OP is a common one and it's solvable in most invoicing tools without changing software.
>
> What you want is two documents from one set of numbers. Internally you itemise everything, because that's how you know the job made money. Externally the customer sees a summary line, "Replace switchboard and install 6 x GPOs as quoted, $2,450 + GST", because an itemised invoice invites the customer to argue about the price of a cable clip.
>
> Most tools do this one of two ways. Either the quote is itemised and the invoice is a single summary line that references the quote number, or the invoice keeps the line items but you group them under one heading with a subtotal and hide the unit prices. Xero does the first cleanly; the field-service apps mostly do the second. Check which your software does before assuming it can't.
>
> The one thing to keep on the customer-facing version whichever way you go: the ABN, the words "Tax invoice", the GST amount shown separately, and a reference back to the accepted quote. That's what the ATO wants on it and it's what stops the "I never agreed to that" conversation. Everything else the customer doesn't need to see.

## 6. Quora — "What are the best software for quotation making?" (disclosed, link OK)

https://www.quora.com/What-are-the-best-software-for-quotation-making
Verified open 2026-09-11, logged in. Latest activity 31 Jul 2026. The
motivational-quotes answer is still there; the top human answers are a bot
list and a five-year-old blinds-software plug. Nothing for a trade business.

> Depends entirely on what "quotation" means for your business, and half the answers here are about literary quotes, so let me split it properly. Disclosure up front: I'm the founder of QuoteMate, a quoting app for Australian tradespeople, so I have a view.
>
> **If you sell a service with a scope and a price** (consulting, agencies, freelancers): you want a proposal tool. PandaDoc, Qwilr and Better Proposals are the usual picks. They're good at layout, e-signature and "the client opened it" tracking. They know nothing about materials.
>
> **If you sell configured products with pricing rules** (bundles, discounts, approvals): that's CPQ. Salesforce CPQ and Oracle CPQ if you're enterprise; Zoho CPQ or Quotient for smaller sales teams. Expensive, and overkill for anyone without a sales team.
>
> **If you're a trade or field business** (electrician, plumber, builder, landscaper): none of the above fits, because your quote is a materials list with quantities and today's supplier prices plus labour, and it has to be built on a phone at the customer's place, not at a desk. The category you want is a quoting app built for trades. In Australia that's what QuoteMate does: describe the job in plain English, it drafts the itemised materials and labour with live supplier pricing, adds GST, and sends a branded PDF the customer can accept online. Free plan, flat fee on the paid tier rather than per user. In the US, Jobber and Housecall Pro are the common picks, though both are job-management platforms priced for crews rather than quoting tools.
>
> Whichever category you're in, run the trial against one test: can you produce a finished, sendable quote for a real job in under ten minutes? If not, the feature list doesn't matter.
>
> https://quotemateapp.au

Why this works: it is the first answer on the thread that separates the
three real categories, it names competitors honestly in each, and the
